# Pogtown — Canonical Character Roster v1.0

The direction: public-domain characters + original Pogtown personalities + a shared JokeBlock comedy engine.

Each actor needs a reusable character package:

- Identity: original literary/cartoon character, or Pogtown original.
- Voice: identifiable vocal reference, plus a licensed or original synthesis voice.
- Body: canonical textured `.glb`, rig, facial expressions and animations.
- Personality graph: motivations, contradictions, fears, relationships, ignorance and characteristic reactions.
- Comedy policy: how that character interprets a news event, writes jokes and interacts with other performers.
- Continuity: recurring jokes, grudges, character development and audience memories.

A public-domain character does not automatically mean a public-domain voice recording or modern character design. The earliest Betty Boop precursor became public domain in the US in 2026, but later Betty Boop designs and trademarks remain protected. Use historical performances as creative references, then create original, distinct voice presets rather than cloning protected performances without permission.

## 1. The primary cast

Proposed canonical personalities — original Pogtown interpretations, not reproduced dialogue.

### 01. Popeye — The working-class philosopher

Comedy: A stubborn manual worker convinced every new technology is trying to put him out of business.
Voice reference: Early sailor vernacular and 1930s Popeye performances; design an original gravelly, muttering, mid-low Atlantic sailor voice.
GLB: Meshy Popeye collection — existing models, including Dockyard Dynamo. Must select an early-version-compatible design.
Joke: “They made artificial intelligence. I'm still waiting for natural intelligence at the docks.”

### 02. Flip the Frog — The optimistic idiot

Comedy: Enthusiastically adopts every trend and never understands the consequences.
Voice reference: 1930 Fiddlesticks era; original excitable high-tenor voice with abrupt squeaks.
GLB: Quaternius animated Frog — verified CC0 animated asset, to customise into Flip's early appearance. ([Poly Pizza](https://poly.pizza))
Joke: “I told ChatGPT I was unemployed. It congratulated me on becoming an entrepreneur!”

### 03. Felix the Cat — The silent schemer

Comedy: A clever, cynical opportunist who solves problems through absurd physical transformations.
Voice reference: No original speaking voice. Invent a soft, dry New York trickster voice, with substantial silent performance.
GLB: Meshy library; look for black vintage cartoon cat, then adapt to the early silhouette.
Joke: Responds to an AI layoffs announcement by literally stealing the letters from the company's sign and selling them.

### 04. Winnie-the-Pooh — The accidental philosopher

Comedy: Gently dismantles extremely complex ideas with apparently foolish questions.
Voice reference: Original British storybook bear — soft, unhurried, thoughtful; not Disney's Pooh voice.
GLB: Meshy library; search `storybook teddy bear`, using Shepard's original illustrations as design context.
Joke: “If the computer knows everything, why does it keep asking me if I'm a robot?”

### 05. Sherlock Holmes — The forensic narcissist

Comedy: Treats ludicrously trivial events as major criminal conspiracies.
Voice reference: Early British stage/radio detective tradition; original precise, dry, educated baritone.
GLB: Meshy library; search `Victorian detective`.
Joke: “Watson, the machine claims to be thinking. Yet it has subscribed to LinkedIn Premium.”

### 06. Betty Boop — The flapper celebrity

Comedy: A witty celebrity navigating absurd modern social expectations and publicity scandals.
Voice reference: 1920s/1930s cabaret vocal mannerisms; original airy, playful mezzo voice. Avoid copying the recognisable later vocal characterisation.
GLB: Meshy library; search `1930 flapper cartoon`, then adapt to Dizzy Dishes rather than the modern design.
Joke: “Darling, AI can replace my face, but can it replace my divorce lawyer?”

### 07. Alice — The sane person in an insane world

Comedy: Ruthlessly exposes contradictions by asking perfectly reasonable questions.
Voice reference: Original curious, articulate young adult British voice, understated and increasingly exasperated.
GLB: Meshy Alice collection — search for book-inspired versions, avoiding later proprietary designs.
Joke: “So the AI is intelligent because people believe it, and people believe it because the AI says so?”

### 08. Count Dracula — The old-money predator

Comedy: A centuries-old aristocrat struggling to understand modern dating, financial markets and technology.
Voice reference: An original restrained Eastern European-inflected aristocratic baritone, not a Bela Lugosi performance clone.
GLB: Poly Pizza vampire/undead assets; select a gothic humanoid base and customise the clothing. ([Poly Pizza](https://poly.pizza))
Joke: “In my day, we simply entered a man's home and drained his life. Now they call it a subscription.”

### 09. Frankenstein's Monster — The AI rights activist

Comedy: An articulate artificial being furious that humanity continues creating intelligent creatures and immediately mistreating them.
Voice reference: Slow, articulate, melancholic original bass; avoid the Karloff film portrayal.
GLB: Quaternius animated Skeleton as a rigged anatomical starting point, or create a bespoke creature. ([Poly Pizza](https://poly.pizza))
Joke: “They gave me consciousness and no employment contract. At least the new robots get charging stations.”

### 10. White Rabbit — The panicking middle manager

Comedy: A bureaucrat who treats every minor inconvenience as a career-ending catastrophe.
Voice reference: Rapid, upper-register, clipped British office-worker voice, breathless and nervous.
GLB: Meshy library; search `Victorian rabbit waistcoat pocket watch`.
Joke: “The AI completed my annual performance review in three seconds. I've been worrying about it for eleven months.”

## 3. Voices

Don't clone historical recordings — historical audio can carry recording rights separate from the underlying character, even when the work is old ([U.S. Copyright Office](https://www.copyright.gov)). Assign an independently created voice to each actor, using references as performance direction.

For every character, produce a 60–120-second voice reference script containing conversational speech, sarcasm, anger, excitement, whispered asides, shouting and laughter. Record with a consenting performer or generate from a commercially permitted synthetic voice. Then use that approved voice as the reference for any cloning or voice adaptation.

Canonical voice profile fields: `voice_id` (e.g. `pog.popeye.v1`), register, texture, rhythm, accent, emotional_range, signature_behavior, reference_rights, tts_reference_audio. See `docs/VOICE-BANK.md` for the render contract (`data/voice-bank/<voice-id>/`).

Critical: do not bake voice personality entirely into the audio. The CharacterGraph should also supply rhythm, verbal habits, emotional triggers and dialogue style, so TTS providers can be swapped without losing the character.

## 4. How assets become canonical Pogtown actors

Identity + provenance (original source, version restrictions, ownership) → performance GLB (rig, expressions, actions) → voice package (TTS, timing, emotional presets) → CharacterGraph (beliefs, desires, blind spots, memories) → JokeBlock adapter (comic operators, scoring, callbacks) → PogActor runtime (performs, reacts, debates, remembers, publishes clips).

The runtime refers to the same `actor_id` (`pog.*` namespace) whether the character is in a stand-up show, reacting to news, playing Mafia, being interviewed, or merchandised. See `docs/POGTOWN-ENGINE.md` for the figgsite↔freaktown contract. Freak-native character layout: `docs/CHARACTER_PACK.md` (`freaks/<slug>/`).

## 5. Asset acquisition priorities

Start with eight, not twenty: Popeye (jobs material, Meshy), Flip (Quaternius Frog CC0), Felix (Animated Cat CC0), Dracula (vampire collection), White Rabbit (Animated Bunny CC0), Frankenstein's Monster (animated creature base), Bartholomew III (Animated Human CC0), The Algorithm (Animated Robot CC0).

Meshy Community models are described as CC0 with GLB downloads — verify each model's IP status and riggability individually; none has been technically tested here yet. US public-domain status is not universal clearance — attach jurisdiction-specific review before commercial launch.

Make Popeye, Flip, Dracula, Frankenstein's Monster and The Algorithm the first ensemble: their attitudes toward AI are so incompatible that one headline creates natural conflict. Immediate missing step: asset QA — inspect GLBs, confirm licences, validate rigs, facial controls and mouth animation.

Answering Your "Remaining Challenges": How We Learn the New Parameters
You correctly identified that this new, more sophisticated architecture has new parameters that need to be learned (A, B, decay factors, etc.). We do not hand-tune them. Everything is learned from the single, unified RelationalLoss function.

How do we learn the A & B matrices? Your InertiaPredictor and AdaptationPredictor are small neural networks. Their weights are part of the overall system's parameters. When we backpropagate the RelationalLoss, the gradients flow back and update these predictors. The system learns: "The last time I used high-inertia matrices in a 'new' relationship phase, the outcome was poor (high loss). I will adjust the weights of the InertiaPredictor to output lower inertia in that context next time."

How do we learn the decay factor? Your InteractionImportanceDetector is a learned classifier. We train it on interaction data. An interaction followed by high user engagement or positive explicit feedback is labeled as importance=1.0. An interaction that's ignored or gets negative feedback is labeled importance=0.1. The detector learns to predict the importance of any new interaction, which in turn determines the decay factor for the LoRA update.
